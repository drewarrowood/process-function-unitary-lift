Unique completion, unitary processes, and the embeddability gap

What is shown, what is only written, and what is not claimed.

Abstract. This paper separates three claims that the literature on causal loops has run together. The first is logical. A process function is a map from outputs to inputs with exactly one fixed point under every local intervention. We prove, and Lean checks, that distinct output strings then leave some party who disagrees in the output and agrees in the function value, and that the source-and-sink extension of Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych is a bijection on bits. The second is quantum. The written argument is that this witness is the off-diagonal of the gram matrix of the permutation unitary, so the induced source-to-sink map is unitary for every local unitary, and instruments follow by Stinespring. That identification is not in the kernel. The third is geometric. Ordered process functions embed as channels in Minkowski spacetime. Cyclic ones, of which Lugano is the witness, have a reversible extension and a written purification, and no known local Hamiltonian on a wormhole whose solutions are exactly their fixed points. Post-selection simulates the list. It does not embed it.

1. What this paper shows, and what it does not

The demand in Faye's Stanford entry, revised 28 October 2025, is still open in the form he states it. A logic of backward causation has to block grandfather cases and bootstrap cases without collapsing into the stipulation that only consistent loops occur. This paper answers one reading of that demand and refuses two others.

Shown. If a classical map has a unique fixed point for every local deterministic intervention, then it cannot have two distinct output strings that agree in the function wherever they differ. The proof builds the intervention that would otherwise fix both. On bits, the source-and-sink extension (o, e) maps to (w(o) XOR e, o) is a bijection, with the inverse written down. Both are theorems in lean/Full.lean, Lean 4.34.1, no unfinished proof. An earlier file checks Lugano on all 64 binary interventions.

Written, not checked. Contracting the permutation unitary against local unitaries, the gram entry factors into row overlaps against the shift w(s') minus w(s). The witness party makes an off-diagonal factor the inner product of two distinct rows. If those rows are orthonormal, the induced map is unitary. A local instrument is a unitary on an ancilla followed by a partial trace. The kernel does not have the sum, a general complex unitary, or Stinespring.

Not claimed. A metric. A decision about freedom. A solution of the Leifer-Pusey no-go. An embedding of Lugano as the fixed-point set of a wormhole Hamiltonian. A refereed theorem.

2. Bilking, and why a slogan is not a criterion

The cheapest objection to backward causation is lexical. A cause is what comes before, so an effect that precedes its cause is a contradiction in terms. Dummett's 1954 paper to the Aristotelian Society, and the 1964 essay Bringing about the Past, treat that as a stipulation about English. One can reserve the word for the earlier relatum and still ask whether a later event is necessary for an earlier one. Faye still takes the temporal order to be contingent. The entry also still treats the consistency paradoxes as unresolved. That is the right diagnosis of the literature.

Black's bilking argument, Analysis 1956, survives the lexical move. Suppose an earlier event E is the effect of a later event C. An agent who observes E can, it seems, intervene so that C does not occur. If the intervention succeeds, C was not necessary for E. If it cannot succeed, the agent lacks an ability the local facts appear to leave open. Horwich later pressed the same point as a constraint: the theory must say what happens to the intervention, not merely announce that paradoxical interventions do not arise.

Lewis's 1976 paper is a reply about modality. One can kill one's grandfather relative to the local facts. One cannot, relative to the whole past, because the past already contains the failure. The two uses of can are not in conflict once their contexts are distinguished. That dissolves a verbal paradox. It does not say which global histories the laws admit. The slogan that only consistent loops occur has the same defect until consistency is a property of the admitted maps, rather than a filter applied after the solutions have been written down.

3. The spacetime problem

Godel's 1949 solution of Einstein's equation has closed timelike curves through every event. It removes the comfort that the field equation itself forbids loops. Morris, Thorne and Yurtsever, in 1988, showed that a traversable wormhole, held open by stress-energy that violates the averaged null energy condition, can be converted into a time machine by relative motion of the mouths. The conversion is a construction plus an exotic-matter assumption. It is not a theorem that such matter exists. It is a theorem that if it does, the Cauchy problem changes character.

Friedman, Morris, Novikov, Echeverria, Klinkhammer, Thorne and Yurtsever stated the changed problem in 1990. Data admissible on a partial Cauchy surface can fail to extend, or can extend in more than one way, once a system interacts with its own past. Novikov's self-consistency principle says that only globally self-consistent solutions occur. Inconsistent data are assigned measure zero. Nothing reaches in to stop the traveller. The contradictory solutions are not among the solutions.

The billiard makes the underdetermination explicit. Echeverria, Klinkhammer and Thorne, in 1991, integrated a hard sphere in a wormhole that takes the sphere backward in time. Some trajectories have one self-consistent continuation, some have two, and some appear to have none. Carlini, Frolov, Mensky, Novikov and Solodukhin derived a version of self-consistency from stationarity of the action and found the same ill-posedness. Earman's 1995 book is the summary that still holds. A consistency condition imposed on the solution set after the dynamics have been solved is a selection rule, not a dynamics.

That is the classical shape of the gap. A process function wants multiplicity one for every intervention. The billiard does not supply it. The lift below is a statement about processes, not a Hamiltonian embedding. We return to that distinction in section 8, where it is the whole of the embeddability gap.

4. Three quantum replies

Deutsch's 1991 model asks for a fixed point of the partial trace around the curve. In finite dimension a fixed point exists, so no particular action is forbidden, and the grandfather paradox becomes a mixed state. The cost is nonlinearity. The model clones unknown states. Aaronson and Watrous showed in 2009 that the computational power is PSPACE. Bennett, Leung, Smith and Smolin argued that the power is an artefact of demanding a fixed point for a distribution an ordinary preparation would not produce. Tolksdorf and Verch showed in 2018 that the Deutsch condition can be met to arbitrary precision in quantum field theory with no closed curve. A Deutsch fixed point is not diagnostic of a loop.

Lloyd, Maccone and collaborators replace the fixed point by post-selection. Inconsistent branches fail the post-selection. The grandfather paradox is resolved by the experiment not happening. Process functions sit inside this class. The event list appears conditional on success. That is a simulation. It has the same shape as a measure-zero axiom. The theory contains the bad branch and declines to count it.

The third reply is the process matrix of Oreshkov, Costa and Brukner (2012). A process maps local instruments to probabilities and is required to return a probability for every choice of instruments. No global causal order is assumed. The quantum switch lives in this class and does not violate a causal inequality. Processes that do violate causal inequalities also live here. The formalism is not a claim that nature realises the violating processes. It is a claim that local quantum theory does not, by itself, force a global order.

5. Process functions

A process function is a map from the tuple of outputs to the tuple of inputs such that every tuple of local functions has exactly one fixed point under composition. Baumeler and Wolf (2016) described the classical polytope. Two parties are not enough for a classical causal inequality. Three are. The Lugano function, w(a, b, c) = (not b and c, not c and a, not a and b), is the witness. Each input depends on the other two outputs. Every one of the 64 deterministic binary interventions has exactly one fixed point. The twelve binary two-party process functions are all causally ordered: four with no signalling, four signalling one way, four the other. Classical consistency does not generate a two-party loop. It does generate a three-party one.

No fixed point is the grandfather case. More than one is the bootstrap. The freedom in the quantifier is the no-new-physics principle of Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych. Any operation possible in an ordinary region remains possible in a region that does not itself contain the closed curve. Consistency may not forbid the operation. It may only constrain the global solution. Dourdent, Leitherer, Boghiu, Simonov, Kunjwal and Acin restate the clause without quantifying over interventions. A list of events is the event list of a process function exactly when it is complete and pairwise exclusive. That is a multipartite form of Specker's principle, from the 1960 Dialectica paper. It characterises logical consistency. It does not say which lists embed in a spacetime. They leave that open on purpose.

6. What the kernel accepted

Reversibility is not free on the original alphabet. Lugano is two-to-one. None of the twelve binary two-party process functions equals its input-output reverse. Theorem 2 of Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych (2019) restores reversibility by a source, a region with trivial input, and a sink, a region with trivial output. The form used here is U|o, e> = |w(o) + e, o>. The inverse reads the sink as the output string and the source as the difference between the input and w(o).

The file lean/Full.lean, compiled with Lean 4.34.1, no Mathlib, no unfinished proof, contains three theorems. process_disagreement: for every number of parties, on bits, if w is a process function and s differs from s', some party disagrees in the output bit and agrees in the value of w. The proof builds the local intervention that would otherwise fix both strings. Uniqueness forbids it. extend_left_inv and extend_right_inv: the map (o, e) maps to (w(o) XOR e, o) is a bijection. The inverse is written down. On the computational basis that bijection is a permutation unitary. hadamard_rows_orthogonal is only an integer witness that the rows (1, 1) and (1, -1) have dot product zero. It is not a complex Hadamard, which needs the square root of two.

lean/Lift.lean proves the same exclusivity lemma for an arbitrary party type and an arbitrary alphabet with decidable equality, not only bits. lean/Cancel.lean checks, by finite evaluation, that Lugano has one fixed point under all 64 interventions, that the witness property holds on all pairs, and that the identity loop and the three-cycle fail both. That count is not the general lemma. The general lemma is process_disagreement.

7. The written cancellation

A unitary on a fixed basis is not yet a quantum process. Parties must insert instruments off that basis. The written theorem is this. For any local unitaries, possibly on a local ancilla, the source-to-sink operator induced by U is unitary. Every tuple of local instruments therefore induces a completely positive trace-preserving map from source to sink. Fixing the source at the identity recovers w.

The matrix element is the product, over parties, of the amplitude for the local unitary to take the shifted source bit to the sink bit. Change variables by i_k = w_k(s) + e_k. The gram matrix factors across parties. The (s, s') entry is a product, over k, of the overlap between row s_k and row s'_k shifted by delta_k = w_k(s') - w_k(s). If s = s', every shift vanishes and each factor is a row norm. If s differs from s', the lemma supplies a party with shift zero and distinct row indices. That factor is the inner product of two distinct rows of a unitary, ancilla included, hence zero. So the gram matrix is the identity. A local instrument is a unitary on a local ancilla followed by a partial trace.

The same identity is why a non-process fails. The swap and the identity loop have distinct strings with no such party. Their dilations are not trace-preserving. Unitarity of the local gates is not enough. The process-function clause is what kills the shift. This paragraph is the written step. The kernel has the witness party and a bijection. It does not have the sum, the change of variables as a theorem about the contracted operator, or row orthonormality of a general complex unitary. Stinespring is not formalised.

8. Novelty, stated narrowly

The result is not that Lugano has a unitary extension. Araujo, Feix, Navascues and Brukner showed that in 2017, and Vanrietvelde, Ormrod, Kristjansson and Barrett wrote it as a routed circuit in 2022. Those are one function. The 2019 paper proves the classical reversible extension and says the quantum step is open. Daher Ahmed and Kunjwal, on 30 September 2026, conjecture the universal statement and prove it under mutual exclusivity of control conditions and under unambiguity, neither necessary, and note that the bare permutation unitary may fail to be a valid process. The argument here is that it does not fail, for a process function, and that the sufficient conditions are not used. The reason is the forbidden double fixed point, which is exactly the off-diagonal the contraction needs to kill. That identification is written. The half of it that says a double fixed point is forbidden is checked.

Only consistent loops occur can mean three things. It can mean that inconsistent solutions are discarded after the dynamics. It can mean that a post-selection keeps them out of the sample. Or it can mean that the admitted set is the set of maps with unique fixed points, and that this set is already closed under quantum instruments via a dilation one can write down. The third reading is the one this argument supports, if the written step is granted. It does not choose between Price and Maudlin. Price argued in 2012 that a time-symmetric ontology should be retrocausal if the quantum state is real. Leifer and Pusey replaced that assumption with lambda-mediation and derived a timelike Bell factorization, which sequential measurements violate. Maudlin replied that the operational time-reverse of an experiment is not always an experiment of the same kind. The dilation here is adjacent. It removes the reply that the cyclic model dies when instruments become unitary. It does not settle the ontology.

9. The embeddability gap

The gap is physical, not algebraic. A process function is a list of events. A spacetime is a metric. Nothing in the cancellation turns one into the other. Three different things have been called an embedding, and only one of them is the gap.

A post-selected circuit can make the Lugano event list appear in the runs that survive. That is a simulation. Process functions sit inside post-selected closed timelike curves. The event list is conditional on the post-selection succeeding. It is not an account of why the inconsistent branch was never a physical option.

The source-and-sink bijection embeds the function in a larger function. That is reversibility. The 2019 paper realises it by interactions among bounded regions. It does not exhibit a metric. Adding a source and a sink supplies two extra regions, not a throat. Lugano is two-to-one on its original alphabet, so the input-output relation is not the graph of a reversible Hamiltonian flow on those three bits. Lean checked the restored bijection. The extra coordinates are operational boundaries.

A Lorentzian embedding would be a local flow on a fixed metric whose solutions, for every local intervention, are exactly the fixed points of the process function. Only this third reading is the gap Dourdent and collaborators left open.

The standard wormhole does not close it. The billiard has the wrong multiplicity. Deutsch's model keeps the multiplicity and averages it. Post-selection throws the extra solutions away. Neither is an embedding. A Hamiltonian tuned so that one interaction has a unique continuation does not meet the quantifier over every local operation.

The ordered case does close. The twelve binary two-party process functions are channels. A channel embeds in Minkowski spacetime as free propagation from the earlier party to the later one, with the local instrument at the later end. No wormhole is required. Baumeler and Wolf proved that a classical violation of a causal inequality needs three parties, so every two-party process function is of this kind.

The cyclic case does not close by the same route. Lugano violates a causal inequality, so no wiring in a definite order, and no mixture of orders, reproduces it if the settings are free. Vilasini and Renner (arXiv:2203.11245) show that indefinite causal order cannot be implemented with spacetime-localised systems in an acyclic spacetime without violating relativistic causality, and that any implementation can be fine-grained to a fixed acyclic order. A metric with closed timelike curves can, in principle. What is missing is a local Hamiltonian on such a metric whose consistency equation is the process-function equation and whose multiplicity is one for every intervention. No other one than the billiard is known, and the billiard fails. The purification does not supply that Hamiltonian. It is a third object, beside the simulation and the embedding.

10. What a referee should accept, and what a referee should not

A referee who accepts the lemma should accept that a double fixed point is forbidden, and that the source-and-sink extension is a bijection on bits. A referee who wants the sentence every process function purifies as a kernel-checked theorem about complex unitaries should not find it here. A referee who wants a spacetime should look elsewhere. Unique completion answers the logical half of the demand in Faye's entry. It does not answer the geometric half. Unique completion is not an open choice, and it is not a solution of the Leifer-Pusey no-go.
