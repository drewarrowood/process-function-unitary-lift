Unique completion, the unitary lift, and what is not a metric

The displays use the notation of the sketch: exclusive or, the fixed point in the output, and the source-and-sink pair. No external script.

1. Register

Claim A is checked. On bits, a process function cannot send two distinct output strings that agree in the function wherever they differ, and the source-and-sink extension is a bijection. Lean 4.34.1 accepted both in lean/Full.lean.

Claim B is written. That witness is the cancellation in the source-to-sink gram matrix. Instruments follow by Stinespring, cited and not formalised.

Claim C is a split. Ordered process functions embed as channels. The cyclic case has a simulation and a reversible extension, and no known local Hamiltonian on a wormhole whose solutions are exactly its fixed points.

2. The objects, in the paper's notation

A process function w sends an output string to an input string. An intervention f is a tuple of local functions from a party's input to that party's output. The fixed point is in the output:

o = f(w(o)). (1)

w is a process function when every f has exactly one solution of (1).

The Lugano function, the three-party witness, is

w(a, b, c) = (¬b ∧ c, ¬c ∧ a, ¬a ∧ b). (2)

Each input depends on the other two outputs. The image has four elements out of eight, so (2) is two-to-one. Reversibility is not free on those three bits.

The source-and-sink extension, the map Lean checked, is

(o, e) ↦ (w(o) XOR e, o). (3)

The inverse, also checked, is

(i, s) ↦ (s, i XOR w(s)). (4)

On bits, XOR cancels, so (4) after (3) and (3) after (4) are the identity. A finite bijection is a permutation unitary on the computational basis. That is representation, not yet a quantum process. The ket form U|o, e⟩ = |w(o) ⊕ e, o⟩ is the same map as (3), written on the basis.

3. Sketch of the proof, matching (1)–(4)

Lemma. If w is a process function and s ≠ s', some party k has

sk ≠ s'k   and   wk(s) = wk(s'). (5)

Otherwise the local function that sends wk(s) to sk and wk(s') to s'k is well defined, and both strings solve (1) for that one intervention. The swap fails (5): under w(a, b) = (b, a), negation on each side fixes both (0, 1) and (1, 0). Lean accepted (5) as process_disagreement.

Contract (3) with a local unitary Vk at each party. The source-to-sink amplitude is

Ms,e = ∏k ⟨sk| Vk |wk(s) XOR ek⟩. (6)

Change variables by the party's source symbol,

ik = wk(s) XOR ek. (7)

The shift between the two output strings is

δk = wk(s') XOR wk(s). (8)

The gram entry factors:

(MM†)s,s' = ∏k ⟨row sk | row s'k shifted by δk⟩. (9)

If s = s', every shift in (8) vanishes and each factor in (9) is a row norm, hence 1. If s ≠ s', (5) supplies a party with δk = 0 and distinct rows, so that factor is 0. Therefore

MM† = I. (10)

(10) is written, not kernel-checked. A local instrument is a unitary on an ancilla followed by a partial trace. Fixing the source at the zero string in (3) recovers w, because the input is then w(o) and the sink is o. The integer rows (1, 1) and (1, −1) have dot product 0. A normalised Hadamard needs √2 and is not in the file.

4. What would be new, and what is not a metric

The 2019 paper proves the classical extension and leaves the quantum step open. (3) and (4) are that extension on bits, checked. (5) through (10) are the candidate for the open step, written. Araújo, Feix, Navascués and Brukner quantize (2) only. Daher Ahmed and Kunjwal prove a sufficient condition this sketch does not use.

A channel, the ordered case, is

iB = w(oA),   iA independent of oB, (11)

or the reverse. (11) embeds as free propagation. (2) violates a causal inequality, so no wiring of that form embeds it. The billiard has the wrong multiplicity. (3) supplies two extra regions, not a metric.
