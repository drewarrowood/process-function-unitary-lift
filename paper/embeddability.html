The embeddability gap

The gap is physical, not algebraic. A process function is a list of events. A spacetime is a metric. Nothing in the cancellation turns one into the other.

Three things that have been called an embedding

A post-selected circuit can make the Lugano event list appear in the runs that survive. That is a simulation. Lloyd, Maccone and collaborators replace the fixed point by post-selection. Process functions sit inside that class. The event list appears conditional on the post-selection succeeding. It is not an account of why the inconsistent branch was never a physical option.

The source-and-sink bijection of Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych embeds the function in a larger function. That is reversibility. The 2019 paper realises the reversible extension by interactions among bounded regions. It does not exhibit a metric.

A Lorentzian embedding would be a local flow on a fixed metric whose solutions, for every local intervention, are exactly the fixed points of the process function. Only the third is the gap.

Why the standard wormhole does not close it

Echeverria, Klinkhammer and Thorne integrated the hard-sphere billiard on the Morris–Thorne wormhole and found initial data with one self-consistent continuation, data with two, and data with none apparent. A process function demands exactly one fixed point for every intervention. The billiard has the wrong multiplicity, so the Lugano list is not the fixed-point set of that flow. Deutsch’s model keeps the multiplicity and averages it. Post-selection throws the extra solutions away. Neither is an embedding.

There is a sharper obstruction for an autonomous classical flow. Lugano is two-to-one on its original alphabet. The input-output relation is not the graph of a reversible Hamiltonian flow on those three bits. The source and the sink restore a bijection, and Lean checked that bijection. The extra coordinates are operational boundaries, not a throat.

The ordered case does close

The twelve binary two-party process functions are channels. Four have no signalling, four signal one way, four the other. A channel embeds in Minkowski spacetime as free propagation from the earlier party to the later one, with the local instrument at the later end. No wormhole is required. Baumeler and Wolf already proved that a classical violation of a causal inequality needs three parties, so every two-party process function is of this kind.

The cyclic case does not close by the same route

Lugano violates a causal inequality, so no wiring of the three parties in a definite order, and no mixture of orders, reproduces it if the settings are free. A fixed metric with a definite causal order cannot embed it. A metric with closed timelike curves can, in principle. What is missing is a local Hamiltonian on such a metric whose consistency equation is the process-function equation and whose multiplicity is one for every intervention. The billiard is a local Hamiltonian and has the wrong multiplicity. No other one is known. Adding a source and a sink does not supply the metric. It supplies two extra regions.

Dourdent and collaborators leave the physical side open on purpose. The pairwise-exclusive complete event list characterises logical consistency. It does not say which of those lists embed in a spacetime, or arise as the fixed points of a dynamical feedback. Physical realizability of the Lugano process remains an open question in that literature.

What the attempt to close it comes to

The gap splits. Ordered process functions embed as channels. Cyclic ones have a unitary purification, in the written argument, and a reversible extension, which Lean checked as a bijection on bits, and no known local flow on a wormhole whose solutions are exactly their fixed points. The purification does not close that. It is a third object, beside the simulation and the embedding.

The attempt does not produce a metric. It produces an obstruction for the standard billiard, and a positive embedding only for the causally ordered case. The cyclic case stays open.
