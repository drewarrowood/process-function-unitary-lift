What the equations are doing

The integrated argument, with a paragraph under each display. On bits, plus and minus are exclusive or. No external script.

Shown. The exclusivity lemma, and the source-and-sink bijection, on bits. Written. The gram cancellation. Not claimed. A metric, a wormhole Hamiltonian for Lugano, the Leifer–Pusey no-go, freedom.

1. The fixed point

o = f(w(o)). (1)

A party has an input and an output. The process function w reads the outputs and writes the inputs. An intervention f is a choice, at each party, of a function from that party's input to that party's output. Composing them, the outputs have to come back as the outputs. Equation (1) is that loop. A process function is a w for which every f has exactly one solution o. No solution is the grandfather case: the intervention cannot be completed. Two solutions is the bootstrap: the loop has not decided which history occurs. The clause is a restriction on the admitted maps, not a filter applied after the solutions have been written down.

2. Lugano

w(a, b, c) = (¬b ∧ c, ¬c ∧ a, ¬a ∧ b). (2)

Three bits. The first input is true only when b is false and c is true, and the other two inputs are the same pattern, cycled. Each input depends on the other two outputs, so there is no party who can be placed earlier than the others. All sixty-four deterministic interventions have one solution of (1). The image has four strings out of eight, so (2) is two-to-one. Reversibility is not free on these three bits. The twelve binary two-party process functions are channels. A classical violation of a causal inequality needs three parties, so (2) is the smallest cyclic witness.

3. The extension and its inverse

U |o, e⟩ = |w(o) + e, o⟩. (3)

U−1 |i, s⟩ = |s, i + w(s)⟩. (4)

The source e is a region with no input. The sink is the second slot of the output, which records o and has no further output. Equation (3) hides w(o) under the source by addition, and writes the output string into the sink. Equation (4) reads the sink as the output and the source as the difference between the input and w of that output. On bits the sum is exclusive or, so adding w(s) twice cancels, and (4) after (3) is the identity for every w, process function or not. Lean checked both compositions. A bijection of a finite set is a permutation unitary on the computational basis. That is representation. It is not yet a claim that parties may insert instruments off that basis.

4. The lemma

sk ≠ s′k and wk(s) = wk(s′). (5)

If two output strings differ, some party disagrees in the output and agrees in the value of w. Suppose not. Then wherever the function values agree, the strings already agree, so one can define a local function that sends wk(s) to sk and wk(s′) to s′k. Both strings then solve (1) for that one intervention. A process function cannot have that. The swap is the witness the other way: under w(a, b) = (b, a), negation on each side fixes both (0, 1) and (1, 0). Lean accepted (5) as process_disagreement, on bits, for every number of parties. This is the checked half of the argument.

5. The amplitude, the shift, and the gram matrix

Ms,e = ∏k ⟨sk| Vk |wk(s) + ek⟩. (6)

Each party inserts a unitary Vk, possibly on a local ancilla. Equation (6) is the amplitude for the source e, fed through (3) and through those unitaries, to come out as the sink string s. It factors because the parties act separately. The ket wk(s) + ek is the input (3) writes at party k.

δk = wk(s′) − wk(s). (7)

The shift is how much the two output strings disagree in the function at party k. Change variables by ik = wk(s) + ek. Exclusive or with a fixed string is a bijection of the source, so the sum over e is a sum over i. After that change, the overlap at party k is between row sk and row s′k shifted by (7).

(MM†)s,s′ = ∏k ⟨row sk | row s′k shifted by δk⟩. (8)

MM† = I. (9)

If s = s′, every shift vanishes and each factor is a row norm, hence 1. If s differs from s′, (5) supplies a party whose shift is zero and whose rows differ. That factor is the inner product of two distinct rows of a unitary, and vanishes. One zero factor kills the product, so the gram matrix is the identity. That is (9). It is written. The kernel has (5) and the bijection. It does not have the sum, or row orthonormality over the complex numbers. A local instrument is a unitary on an ancilla followed by a partial trace. Fixing the source at 0 in (3) recovers w, because the input is then w(o) and the sink is o.

6. A channel, and why Lugano is not one

iB = w(oA), iA independent of oB. (10)

Equation (10) is a channel from A to B. It embeds in Minkowski spacetime as free propagation from the earlier party to the later one. No wormhole is required. Equation (2) has no such wiring if the settings are free: each input depends on the other two outputs. Post-selection can make the event list appear in the runs that survive. That is a simulation. Equations (3) and (4) are reversibility, not a metric. A Lorentzian embedding would be a local flow whose solutions, for every intervention, are exactly the solutions of (1). The billiard has the wrong multiplicity. No other local Hamiltonian is known. The cyclic case stays open.

7. What would be new

Araújo, Feix, Navascués and Brukner quantized (2) in 2017. The 2019 paper proves (3) classically and leaves the quantum step open. Daher Ahmed and Kunjwal conjectured the general lift and proved it under two sufficient conditions this sketch does not use. The new step is that the forbidden double fixed point, (5), is the off-diagonal in (8). The prohibition is checked. The identification is written. Unique completion answers the logical half of the demand in Faye's entry. It does not answer the geometric half.
