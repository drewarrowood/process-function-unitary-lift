Every process function lifts to a unitary process

A philosophical and formal note on what logical consistency of a causal loop already buys, once a source and a sink are allowed.

Abstract. A process function is a classical map from the outputs of several parties to their inputs such that every choice of local deterministic interventions has exactly one fixed point. Such maps are the deterministic skeletons of logically consistent processes without a predefined causal order. Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych showed that every process function extends to a bijection by the addition of a source and a sink, and left open whether every such bijection quantizes to a valid unitary quantum process. Daher Ahmed and Kunjwal conjectured that it does. This note gives an argument that it does. The dilation is the permutation unitary of the source-and-sink extension. For any tuple of local unitaries, the induced source-to-sink operator is unitary, because a process function cannot have two distinct global strings that agree on the output of the process wherever they disagree as strings. Local instruments then induce a completely positive trace-preserving map by the usual dilation. Fixing the source at the identity recovers the original process function. The philosophical claim is narrower than the formalism: consistency, in the fixed-point sense, is already a quantization condition. It does not have to be imposed again as a measure-zero axiom on paradoxical histories.

1. The philosophical problem the formalism is answering

Backward causation has never been short of paradoxes. It has been short of a criterion that says which loops are admissible without putting the answer in by hand.

The bilking argument, in Black's form, says that if an earlier event is supposed to be the effect of a later one, an intervention after the earlier event can prevent the later one, so the later event was not necessary for the earlier. The grandfather paradox says that an agent equipped to kill an earlier self both can and cannot. The bootstrap says that information, or a billiard trajectory, can come from nowhere, because the loop is its own source. Newcomb-type cases add a threat to freedom: if the earlier state already reflects the later choice, the later choice looks idle.

The standard replies are real, and they are all filters. Lewis's contextual “can” makes the ability compossible with the local facts and not with the whole past. Novikov's self-consistency principle, stated for closed timelike curves by Friedman, Morris, Novikov, Thorne and collaborators in 1990, says that only globally self-consistent solutions get nonzero measure. Greenberger and Svozil observe that certain quantum feedback arrangements cancel the inconsistent paths. Each of these excludes the bad case. None of them derives the exclusion from the structure of the loop. The bad histories are removed by a stipulation, or by a dynamical accident of one model. A reader who wants to know what consistency is, rather than how a particular theory enforces it, is entitled to be unsatisfied.

The process-function clause is an attempt at the missing criterion. A classical environment interacting with several localized parties is logically consistent if and only if, whatever the parties do, the composed map has exactly one fixed point. No fixed point is the grandfather case: the intervention has nowhere to land. More than one is the bootstrap: the loop underdetermines its own contents. The 2026 characterization of Dourdent, Leitherer, Boghiu, Simonov, Kunjwal and Acín says the same thing without recursion. A list of events is the event list of a process function exactly when it is complete and pairwise exclusive. If any two of several questions can be answered jointly, so can all of them. That is a multipartite form of Specker's principle, and it is a constraint on the process, not an extra axiom that paradoxical histories have measure zero.

This is already a philosophical result, before any quantization. It says that cyclic causation is not the enemy. Cyclic signaling is. A party may sit in the causal future of another and in its causal past, provided the dependence does not let a local choice write a contradiction into the loop. The Lugano function is the standard witness. Each party's input depends on the outputs of the other two, the dependence graph is a cycle, a causal inequality is violated, and every deterministic intervention still has exactly one fixed point. The twelve binary two-party process functions, by contrast, are all causally ordered. Classical consistency does not generate a two-party loop. It does generate a three-party one. The difference is not a verbal one.

2. Why quantization is the next philosophical question

A criterion for classical loops is not yet a theory of physical loops. The world, if it has anything like a closed timelike curve, or anything like an indefinite causal order, is not a Boolean circuit. The natural demand is that the same consistency survive the passage to quantum instruments.

There are two bad ways to impose that demand. The first is Deutsch's fixed-point condition on the density operator around the curve. In finite dimension a fixed point always exists, so no particular action is forbidden, and the grandfather paradox becomes a mixed state. The cost is nonlinearity, and the model clones unknown states. Tolksdorf and Verch showed that the same fixed point can be met to arbitrary precision in ordinary quantum field theory on a spacetime with no closed timelike curve at all. A Deutsch fixed point is therefore not diagnostic of a loop. The second bad way is post-selection. Process functions sit inside post-selected closed timelike curves. The event list appears conditional on the post-selection succeeding. That is a simulation. It is not an account of why the inconsistent branch was never a physical option.

The good demand is unitarity of a dilation. Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych proved that every process function extends to a bijection by the addition of a source, a region with trivial input, and a sink, a region with trivial output. The extended process can be read backwards. Reversibility is not free on the original alphabet: the Lugano function is not bijective, and none of the twelve binary two-party process functions equals its input-output reverse. Reversibility is free once the source and the sink are granted. A bijection of a finite set is a permutation unitary. The open question, stated in 2019 and conjectured again on 30 September 2026, is whether that unitary is a valid process: whether parties may insert arbitrary quantum instruments and still induce a completely positive trace-preserving map from source to sink.

Why is this philosophical rather than merely technical? Because it decides whether consistency is a property of the loop or a property we add when we quantize. If the lift fails, classical consistency and quantum consistency come apart, and a process can be free of grandfather and bootstrap failures in the Boolean fragment and still produce a non-physical quantum instrument. If the lift holds, the fixed-point clause was already the quantum condition. The source and the sink are not a trick. They are the admission that a loop, read as a physical process, has a boundary. The past boundary is the source. The future boundary is the sink. Time symmetry, in the thin sense of reversibility, is the existence of the inverse reading. It is not the demand that the bare function be an involution.

3. Time symmetry, retrocausality, and what this does not settle

Huw Price has argued that a time-symmetric ontology for quantum theory should be retrocausal. Leifer and Pusey sharpened the argument so that it does not need the quantum state to be ontic. Under λ-mediation — the ontic state screens the preparation from the later measurement — together with no retrocausality and operational time symmetry, sequential measurements on one system cannot violate a timelike Bell inequality. They do. So a time-symmetric ontology that keeps mediation cannot also refuse influences that depend on later settings.

The lift in this note is not a solution of that no-go. It is adjacent to it. A process function that violates a causal inequality already has each party's input depending on another party's output. The later setting is an argument of the earlier input. λ-mediation, in the strict past-screening sense, fails, and it fails because of the cycle, not because a retrocausal hidden variable was added by hand. The unitary dilation keeps that dependence. It does not derive the timelike Bell violation, and it does not refute Maudlin's objection to the Leifer–Pusey route. What it does is remove one cheap reply. One cannot say that the cyclic classical model is quantum-mechanically idle, a Boolean curiosity that dies when instruments become unitary. If the argument below is right, the cyclic model has a unitary process, and the causal-inequality violation survives as the classical limit of that process.

Two further things are not settled, and should not be smuggled in. The first is spacetime embeddability. The hard-sphere billiard on a wormhole, in the analysis of Friedman, Morris, Novikov, Thorne and collaborators, has data with one self-consistent motion, data with two, and data with possibly none. A process function needs one for every intervention. The unitary lift is a statement in the process-matrix formalism. It is not a local Hamiltonian on a fixed wormhole metric. The second is freedom. Nothing here decides whether an agent whose output is an argument of an earlier input has a choice. The fixed-point clause says the choice, if made, completes uniquely. It does not say the choice was open.

4. The lemma

Let each party have a finite alphabet with a group law, written additively. Bits under XOR are the case that matters. A process function is a map w from output strings to input strings such that for every tuple of local functions, the composition has exactly one fixed point.

Lemma. If w is a process function and s ≠ s', then some party k has s_k ≠ s'_k and w_k(s) = w_k(s').

Suppose not. Then agreement of the process outputs already forces agreement of the strings. Define a local function that sends w_k(s) to s_k and w_k(s') to s'_k, and extend it arbitrarily off those points. The two clauses agree wherever the inputs agree, so the local map is a function. Both strings are then fixed points of one intervention. A process function cannot have that. Distinct candidates for a double fixed point must disagree, at some party, on a coordinate whose process-output value agrees.

This is the philosophical core, stripped of matrices. Consistency forbids a certain kind of pair. The forbidden pair is exactly the pair that would have made the quantum contraction fail.

5. The lift

The reversible extension, Theorem 2 of Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych, is the permutation unitary

U |o, e⟩ = |w(o) + e, o⟩.

The sink records the output string. The source is recovered as the difference between the input and w(o). The operator is unitary for any w. Validity uses the lemma.

Theorem. For any local unitaries, possibly acting on a local ancilla, the source-to-sink operator induced by U is unitary. Every tuple of local instruments therefore induces a completely positive trace-preserving map from source to sink. The Choi operator of U is a valid unitary process. Fixing the source at the identity element of the group recovers w.

The matrix element is the product, over parties, of the amplitude for the local unitary to take the shifted source bit to the sink bit. Summing the source out, the gram matrix factors across parties. On the diagonal, each factor is a row norm. Off the diagonal, the lemma supplies a party at which the shift vanishes and the two sink bits differ, so that factor is the inner product of two distinct rows of a unitary, ancilla included, and vanishes. The gram matrix is the identity.

A local instrument is a unitary on a local ancilla followed by a partial trace. The partial trace of a unitary dilation is completely positive and trace-preserving. That is the whole passage from the combinatorial lemma to a process matrix.

The same identity is why a non-process fails. The swap, and the identity loop, have pairs of distinct strings with no such party. The corresponding factor does not vanish. Unitarity of the local operators does not kill the shift. The process-function clause does.

6. What is interesting, if the lemma stands

The interesting claim is not that a particular function, Lugano, has a unitary extension. That was shown by other means in 2017, and written as a routed circuit in 2022. The interesting claim is that the reason it lifts is the same reason it was consistent. The forbidden double fixed point is the forbidden off-diagonal. Classical consistency and quantum complete positivity are, on this dilation, one fact read twice.

That is the sort of result a foundations audience should want, and the sort it should not overread. It does not make closed timelike curves cheap. It does not choose a spacetime. It does not dissolve bilking as a question about intervention in a relativistic causal structure; bilking, in Black's original setting, is about whether a later event can be necessary for an earlier one when the later event can still be prevented. The process-function reply is that if the prevention is a local function, the composed map has no fixed point, and the process was never admitted. The prevention is not a successful experiment inside the model. It is a description the model excludes. Whether that is an adequate reply to Black depends on whether one thinks consistency conditions may rule out interventions, or only rule out histories given the interventions. The note takes the first reading, and the formalism is built for it.

It also sharpens a slogan that is usually left soft. “Only consistent loops occur” can mean three things. It can mean that we discard inconsistent solutions after solving the dynamics. It can mean that a post-selection keeps them out of the sample. Or it can mean that the set of admitted processes is defined to be the set of maps with unique fixed points, and that this set is already closed under quantum instruments via a dilation we can write down. The third reading is the one this argument supports. It is stronger than the first two, and it is checkable. The combinatorial lemma is the piece an attacker has to break. The linear algebra after it is row orthogonality.

7. What the argument does not do

It does not embed the process in a spacetime. It does not say the bare function is an involution. It is not kernel-checked. Sufficient conditions in the 2026 preprint — mutual exclusivity of control conditions, and unambiguity — are not used. If the lemma stands, those conditions are sufficient but not the reason. If the lemma fails for some alphabet or some reading of the contraction, the conjecture remains a conjecture, and this note is a failed proof rather than a theorem. The honest status is the second of those only if the pair argument is wrong. The pair argument is finite for any fixed alphabet.
