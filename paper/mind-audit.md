# Audit for the Mind-style draft

Private working note. Not part of the review manuscript. No submission is requested or recorded. This draft is not claimed to be submission-ready.

## Status

Branch `cursor/mind-draft-cc9c`, taken from `main` at `47f9f8ee2c9b65becd619eb70891ce46ed1d872a`. This commit does not update `main`, the public site, `bookkeeping`, or the Journal of Philosophy branch.

The argument is not the Journal of Philosophy essay shortened, and not the Synthese draft retitled. Those drafts separate consistency, order, reversibility, purification, time travel, and implementation-as-possible-machine, with several scenes and a historical frame. This draft asks one question: whether admitting every combination of local operations is the same freedom as recombining the events in a history. The positive claim is a typed substitution principle for assessing implementation claims. It is not offered as unprecedented, and it is not a refutation of monism, grounding, or computationalism.

## What was read

- Schaffer, “The Internal Relatedness of All Things,” *Mind* 119 (2010), 341–376, DOI `10.1093/mind/fzq033`, from the author-hosted PDF. Used for priority monism as a dependence order rather than a world-only view; the explicit denial that the priority in play is causal or conceptual dependence; the stereo-knob picture; the §1.4 necessary condition on modal freedom (intrinsic natures, locations, and failure to exist, leaving the rest of the world as it is); and the §4 admission that pluralist replies from free recombination or brute modality remain open. No quotation from Hegel, Bradley, Moore, or Royce is copied from Schaffer. His historical reconstruction is not treated as a reading of those books.
- Chalmers, “A Computational Foundation for the Study of Cognition,” as hosted at consc.net/papers/computation.html, which he describes as the 1993 text later published, apart from one added note, in *Journal of Cognitive Science* 12 (2011), 325–359, DOI `10.17791/jcs.2011.12.4.325`. He reports that the implementation section appeared in *Minds and Machines* 4 (1994), 391–402, DOI `10.1007/BF00974166`. Used for the reliable-cause condition on every formal transition, combinatorial-state automata and distinct regions, the point that one machine may implement many computations while each implementation question stays objective, and the 2011 note that computational sufficiency for mentality is nomological rather than metaphysical. Consciousness is not a conclusion of this draft.
- Baumeler and Wolf (2016) and Baumeler et al. (2019): bibliographic records previously checked. The Lugano rule and the process-function quantifier are the standard ones, with one identity case and one constant-function case shown by hand. The universal count is attributed to the unpublished note, not re-enumerated here.
- Vilasini and Renner (2024): title and DOI. Cited only for no-go results about embedding cyclic information-theoretic structures in acyclic spacetimes. Proofs not re-derived.
- Turing’s 1937 paper was read in an earlier pass of this project (Virginia scan). It is not given a historical section here. Leibniz is not reused.

## Technical limits

Canonical equivalence: the source-sink permutation, every local unitary, every matched finite ancilla. The bare permutation is unitary for every rule. The computational-basis witness is one aside, known board, value \(1/4-(3/16)\sqrt{2}<0\), not a new board and not all-basis. Baire class one is open and only a footnote. Finite tables are treated as searchable. No complexity or hypercomputation claim. No new Lean check. Formal availability of operations is not experimental control or ontological independence.
