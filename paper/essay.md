# Every process function lifts to a unitary process

Draft essay, about the length of a short Foundations of Physics paper. Not submitted. Not refereed.

Repository: https://github.com/drewarrowood/process-function-unitary-lift

## Abstract

A causal loop is usually asked to do four jobs at once: not be a contradiction, not exclude contradictions by a rider about measure, be reversible if the laws are, and survive contact with quantum instruments. This essay separates those jobs. The grandfather case is an intervention with no fixed point. The bootstrap is an intervention with more than one. Novikov's principle, Lewis's contextual can, and post-selection are filters on histories. A process function is the other shape of constraint: a map from outputs to inputs is admitted only when every freely chosen local intervention completes uniquely. Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych showed that every such map extends to a bijection by a source and a sink, and left the quantization open. Daher Ahmed and Kunjwal conjectured that the bijection is a valid unitary process. The sketch given here is that it is. If two output strings differ, some party disagrees in the output and agrees in the function value; otherwise one local function would fix both. Contracting the permutation unitary of the extension against local unitaries, that party contributes the inner product of two distinct rows, which vanishes. The induced source-to-sink map is unitary. Completely positive instruments follow by the usual dilation. The meaning is narrow. Unique completion was already the quantum condition. It does not have to be imposed a second time as a measure-zero axiom. The essay does not embed the process in a spacetime, and it does not decide whether the agent had a choice.

## 1. What the word cause is not doing

The cheapest objection to backward causation is lexical. A cause, it is said, is what comes before, so an effect that precedes its cause is a contradiction in terms. Dummett's 1954 paper to the Aristotelian Society, and the 1964 essay Bringing about the Past, treat that as a stipulation about English, not a discovery about the world. One can reserve the word cause for the earlier relatum and still have a coherent question about whether a later event is necessary for an earlier one. Faye's Stanford entry, substantively revised on 28 October 2025, still takes the temporal order of cause and effect to be contingent. The entry also still treats the consistency paradoxes as unresolved. That is the right diagnosis of the literature, and it is the gap this essay is aimed at.

Black's bilking argument, in Analysis in 1956, is the objection that survives the lexical move. Suppose an earlier event E is supposed to be the effect of a later event C. An agent who observes E can, it seems, intervene so that C does not occur. If the intervention succeeds, C was not necessary for E. If the intervention cannot succeed, the agent lacks an ability that the local facts appear to leave open, and that lack wants an explanation that is not just the fiat that the past is fixed. Horwich later pressed the same point as a constraint on any theory that lets later events bear on earlier ones: the theory must say what happens to the intervention, not merely announce that paradoxical interventions do not arise.

Lewis's 1976 paper on the paradoxes of time travel is the standard reply, and it is a reply about modality, not about dynamics. One can kill one's grandfather, relative to the local facts and the laws in a small region. One cannot, relative to the whole past, because the past already contains the failure. The two cans are not in conflict once their contexts are distinguished. That dissolves a verbal paradox. It does not say which global histories the laws admit, and it does not say why the local ability fails to compose with the rest of the world. A reader who wants a criterion, rather than a reminder that modal contexts shift, is entitled to remain unsatisfied. The same is true of the slogan that only consistent loops occur. Until consistency is a property of the admitted maps, the slogan is a filter applied after the solutions have been written down.

## 2. The spacetime problem

General relativity makes the question a problem in differential equations. Godel's 1949 solution of Einstein's equation has closed timelike curves through every event. The solution is a curiosity of global structure, not a laboratory, but it removes the comfort that the field equation itself forbids loops. Morris, Thorne and Yurtsever, in 1988, showed something sharper for people who want a machine rather than a cosmology. A traversable wormhole, held open by stress-energy that violates the averaged null energy condition, can be converted into a time machine by relative motion of the mouths. The conversion is a construction in classical general relativity plus an exotic-matter assumption. It is not a theorem that such matter exists. It is a theorem that if it does, the Cauchy problem changes character.

Friedman, Morris, Novikov, Echeverria, Klinkhammer, Thorne and Yurtsever stated the changed problem in 1990. For a spacetime that develops closed timelike curves, the initial-value problem on a partial Cauchy surface is not the ordinary one. Data that would be admissible in a globally hyperbolic spacetime can fail to extend, or can extend in more than one way, once the curve lets a system interact with its own past. Their proposal, Novikov's self-consistency principle, is that only globally self-consistent solutions occur. Inconsistent data are assigned measure zero. Nothing reaches in to stop the traveller. The contradictory solutions are simply not among the solutions.

The billiard makes the underdetermination explicit. Echeverria, Klinkhammer and Thorne, in 1991, integrated a hard-sphere ball in a wormhole spacetime that takes the ball backward in time. Some initial trajectories have one self-consistent continuation. Some have two. Some appear to have none. The multiplicity is a property of the classical initial-value problem, not an artefact of quantum indeterminacy. Carlini, Frolov, Mensky, Novikov and Solodukhin later derived a version of self-consistency from stationarity of the action in a model, and found the same ill-posedness: the stationary points do not pick a unique extension. Earman's 1995 book is the philosophical summary that still holds. A consistency condition imposed on the solution set after the dynamics have been solved is not itself a dynamics. It is a selection rule. Selection rules can be true. They are not explanations of why the discarded solutions were not solutions of the equations.

That is the classical shape of the gap Faye's entry still records. A logic of loops has to block grandfather cases and bootstrap cases without collapsing into the stipulation that only consistent loops occur. The stipulation is what Novikov's principle is. The billiard shows why a stipulation about measure does not finish the job: among the consistent solutions, the classical theory often does not choose.

## 3. Three quantum replies

Deutsch's 1991 model is the first quantum reply that became standard. A system travelling on the closed curve is described by a density operator fixed by the partial trace of its interaction with the chronology-respecting systems. In finite dimension a fixed point exists, so no particular action is forbidden. The grandfather paradox becomes a mixed state. The agent who sets out to kill an earlier self is correlated with a branch in which the attempt fails, and the reduced state on the curve is consistent. The cost is nonlinearity of the evolution on the chronology-respecting systems. The model can clone unknown states. Aaronson and Watrous showed in 2009 that the computational power is PSPACE: classical and quantum computing with these curves are equivalent, and both are as strong as polynomial space. Bennett, Leung, Smith and Smolin argued the same year that the power is an artefact. The fixed-point condition is imposed on a distribution that an ordinary preparation, tensored with a maximally mixed state on the curve, would not produce. Tolksdorf and Verch showed in 2018 that the Deutsch condition can be met to arbitrary precision by a quantum field theory on a globally hyperbolic spacetime, with no closed curve at all. A Deutsch fixed point is therefore not diagnostic of a loop. It is a nonlinear closure condition that can be mimicked.

Lloyd, Maccone and collaborators, in 2011, replace the fixed point by post-selection. The circuit is an ordinary quantum circuit with a final projection that keeps only the runs in which the curve matches. Inconsistent branches are the ones that fail the post-selection. The grandfather paradox is resolved by the experiment not happening. An optical experiment illustrated the resolution for one arrangement. The construction is inequivalent to Deutsch's: post-selected closed timelike curves are nonlinear in a different way, and their computational power, in Aaronson's analysis, sits near PP. Process functions, in the sense of the next section, sit inside this class. The event list appears conditional on the post-selection succeeding. That is a simulation. It is the right simulation for a computer-science question about what correlations the list can produce. It is not an account of why the inconsistent branch was never a physical option. Post-selection is a filter on samples. It has the same shape as a measure-zero axiom.

The third reply is the process-matrix formalism of Oreshkov, Costa and Brukner, from 2012. A process is a map from local instruments to joint probabilities. The instruments are ordinary quantum instruments, completely positive and trace-non-increasing. The process is required to return a probability for every choice of instruments, and to return one when the instruments are deterministic. No global causal order is assumed. The quantum switch, in which the order of two operations is controlled by a qubit, lives in this class and does not violate a causal inequality. Processes that violate causal inequalities also live in this class, and no laboratory implementation of those is agreed. The formalism is not a claim that nature realises the violating processes. It is a claim that local quantum theory does not, by itself, force a global order.

## 4. Process functions

Classical deterministic processes in that formalism are process functions. A party has an output, the value it sends, and an input, the value it receives. A process function is a map from the tuple of outputs to the tuple of inputs such that, for every tuple of local functions from inputs to outputs, the composition has exactly one fixed point. Baumeler and Wolf, in 2016, described the polytope of the probabilistic classical processes without predefined order. The deterministic extrema are the process functions, up to a fine-tuning issue they separate off. Two parties are not enough for a classical violation of a causal inequality. Three are. The Lugano function, found by Araujo and Feix and studied by Baumeler and Wolf,

w(a, b, c) = (not b and c, not c and a, not a and b),

is the standard witness. Each party's input depends on the outputs of the other two. The dependence graph is a cycle. A causal inequality is violated. Every one of the 64 deterministic binary interventions has exactly one fixed point. The twelve binary two-party process functions, by contrast, split evenly into no signalling, A to B only, and B to A only. Classical consistency does not generate a two-party loop. It does generate a three-party one.

The fixed-point clause is the criterion the filters do not give. No fixed point is the grandfather case: the intervention has nowhere to land. More than one is the bootstrap: the loop underdetermines its own contents. The freedom in the quantifier is the content of the no-new-physics principle in Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych. Any operation possible in an ordinary region remains possible in a region that does not itself contain the closed curve. Consistency is not allowed to forbid the operation. It is allowed only to constrain the global solution. That is closer to a logic of loops than to a stipulation about measures. An intervention that would bilk is representable. It is excluded because it is not in the set of admitted maps, not because a dynamics was solved and then trimmed.

Dourdent, Leitherer, Boghiu, Simonov, Kunjwal and Acin, in a paper dated 1 October 2026, say the same thing without quantifying over interventions. A list of events is the event list of a process function exactly when it is complete and pairwise exclusive. If any two of several questions can be answered jointly, so can all of them. That is a multipartite form of Specker's principle, from the 1960 Dialectica paper on statements that are not jointly decidable. The 2026 paper is explicit that this characterises logical consistency and does not say which of those lists embed in a spacetime or arise as fixed points of a Hamiltonian flow. The embeddability question is left open on purpose. This essay does not close it.

## 5. Reversibility is a source and a sink

Reversibility is not free on the original alphabet. The Lugano function is two-to-one: its image has four elements out of eight. Of the 256 binary two-party maps, twelve are process functions and none equals its input-output reverse. A demand that the bare function be an involution is a stronger demand than consistency, and the standard example fails it. Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych, Theorem 2 of the 2019 Classical and Quantum Gravity paper, restore reversibility by adding a source, a region with trivial input, and a sink, a region with trivial output. The extended process can be read backwards, and the reversed process stays compatible with reversed local operations. The form used in the sketch below is the permutation

U |o, e> = |w(o) + e, o>.

The inverse reads the sink as the output string and the source as the difference between the input and w(o). A bijection of a finite set is a unitary on the corresponding Hilbert space. The source and the sink are not a bookkeeping trick. They are the admission that a loop, read as a physical process, has a boundary. The past boundary is the source. The future boundary is the sink. Time symmetry, in the thin sense of reversibility, is the existence of the inverse reading. It is not the demand that the bare function equal its own reverse.

A unitary on a fixed basis is not yet a quantum process. Parties must be able to insert instruments that are not diagonal in that basis. A Hadamard is the test case. If the induced map from source to sink ceases to be trace-preserving when a party applies a Hadamard, the classical consistency clause has not lifted, and quantum theory needs an extra filter. If it remains trace-preserving for every instrument, the fixed-point clause was already the quantum consistency clause. One does not add a measure-zero postulate on the way up.

Daher Ahmed and Kunjwal, in a preprint of 30 September 2026, conjecture that every process function purifies to a unitary process. They prove it under mutual exclusivity of control conditions, and under a second sufficient condition, unambiguity. Neither is necessary. They note that the unitary obtained from the reversible extension may not correspond to a valid process. Araujo, Feix, Navascues and Brukner had already given a unitary extension of Lugano in 2017, in the paper that proposes purifiability as a postulate. Vanrietvelde, Ormrod, Kristjansson and Barrett reconstructed that extension as a routed circuit in 2022. Those are existence proofs for one function. The sketch in the next section is the general reason, and it does not use the sufficient conditions.

## 6. Time symmetry and retrocausality

Price has argued, in 2012, that a time-symmetric ontology for quantum theory should be retrocausal, on the assumption that the quantum state is real. Leifer and Pusey, in 2017, replaced that assumption with lambda-mediation: correlations between a preparation and a later measurement are mediated by the ontic state of the system at the intermediate time. No retrocausality, operational time symmetry, and lambda-mediation then imply a timelike Bell factorization. Sequential measurements on one qubit violate the corresponding inequality, up to the Tsirelson bound. So a time-symmetric ontology that keeps mediation cannot also refuse influences that depend on later settings. Maudlin's reply is that the operational time-reverse of an experiment is not always itself an experiment of the same kind. The no-go is conditional on that identification. The exchange does not settle the ontology. It settles that the three assumptions do not hold together if the operational reverse is granted.

The dilation in this essay is adjacent to that argument, not a solution of it. A process function that violates a causal inequality already has each party's input depending on another party's output. The later setting is an argument of the earlier input. Lambda-mediation, in the strict past-screening sense, fails because of the cycle, not because a retrocausal hidden variable was added by hand. Read forwards, the source is an input and the sink is an output. Read backwards, they exchange roles, and the inverse of the permutation unitary is the reversed process. Whether that dependence is the retrocausality Price asked for, or only an operational time-reverse, is a reading of the same operator. The mathematics does not choose the reading. It does remove one cheap reply to the no-go. One cannot say that the cyclic classical model is quantum-mechanically idle, a Boolean curiosity that dies when instruments become unitary. If the sketch is right, the cyclic model has a unitary process, and the causal-inequality violation survives as the classical limit of that process.

## 7. The sketch

Let each party have a finite alphabet with an abelian group law, written additively. Bits under XOR are the case that matters. A process function is a map w from output strings to input strings such that for every tuple of local functions, the composition has exactly one fixed point.

Lemma. If w is a process function and s differs from s', then some party k has distinct components of the two strings and equal components of w.

Suppose not. Then agreement of the process outputs already forces agreement of the strings. Define a local function that sends w_k(s) to s_k and w_k(s') to s'_k, and extend it arbitrarily off those points. The two clauses agree wherever the inputs agree, so the local map is a function. Both strings are then fixed points of one intervention. A process function cannot have that. The swap is the witness in the other direction. The strings (0, 1) and (1, 0), under w(a, b) = (b, a), are both fixed by NOT tensor NOT. The identity loop fails for the same reason. Both are already known not to be process functions. The lemma is the uniqueness clause read contrapositively. It is also free choice doing logical work. The agent at party k is allowed to apply any local function, including the one just defined. If two distinct global strings could be fixed by the same intervention, the process would not have decided which history occurs.

Theorem. Let U be the source-and-sink permutation unitary above. For any local unitaries, possibly acting on a local ancilla, the source-to-sink operator induced by U is unitary. Every tuple of local instruments therefore induces a completely positive trace-preserving map from source to sink. The Choi operator of U is a valid unitary process. Fixing the source at the identity element of the group recovers w.

The matrix element is the product, over parties, of the amplitude for the local unitary to take the shifted source bit to the sink bit. Change variables by i_k = w_k(s) + e_k. The sum over the source factors across parties. The off-diagonal entry is a product of overlaps between a row and a shifted row. If the strings agree, every shift vanishes and each factor is a row norm. If they differ, the lemma supplies a party with shift zero and distinct row indices. That factor is the inner product of two distinct rows of a unitary, ancilla included, hence zero. A local instrument is a unitary on a local ancilla followed by a partial trace. The partial trace of a unitary dilation is completely positive and trace-preserving.

The Hadamard does not open a new paradox. It opens a row of the unitary, and the lemma has already arranged that two candidate histories are orthogonal on some party. Classical consistency and quantum complete positivity are, on this dilation, one fact read twice. A file lean/Cancel.lean was accepted by Lean 4.34.1 for the exclusivity lemma and for the gram cancellation under a hypothesis of row orthonormality. The identification of that gram entry with the concrete contraction against complex local unitaries was not kernel-checked. Row orthogonality is the standard step. The step that could have failed is the combinatorial one.

## 8. What the sketch means, and what it does not

The interesting claim is not that Lugano has a unitary extension. That was shown by other means in 2017 and written as a routed circuit in 2022. The interesting claim is that the reason it lifts is the same reason it was consistent. The forbidden double fixed point is the forbidden off-diagonal. If the lemma stands, the 2026 conjecture is a theorem, and the sufficient conditions in that preprint are sufficient but not the reason.

Three readings of the slogan are now distinguishable. Only consistent loops occur can mean that we discard inconsistent solutions after solving the dynamics. It can mean that a post-selection keeps them out of the sample. Or it can mean that the set of admitted processes is defined to be the set of maps with unique fixed points, and that this set is already closed under quantum instruments via a dilation we can write down. The third reading is the one the sketch supports. It is stronger than the first two, and the combinatorial step is checkable.

The sketch does not embed the process in a spacetime. A unitary process on a finite alphabet is not a solution of Einstein's equation, and it is not a local Hamiltonian flow on a wormhole. The billiard has the wrong multiplicity: a process function needs one continuation for every intervention, and the billiard does not provide that. Post-selection realises the event list conditional on success. Neither is an embedding of the bare list. The 2026 characterisation is explicit that embeddability remains open. This essay leaves it there.

Purifiability in the sense of open quantum problem 43, whether every extensibly causal process is purifiable, is related but not identical. The sketch puts every process function, including those that violate causal inequalities, inside the unitarily extendible fragment. It does not classify processes that are not deterministic classical functions. Araujo, Feix, Navascues and Brukner proposed purifiability as a postulate for which processes are physical. The sketch is consistent with that postulate for this class. It is not a proof of the postulate.

Freedom is not settled. Nothing here decides whether an agent whose output is an argument of an earlier input had a choice. The fixed-point clause says the choice, if made, completes uniquely. It does not say the choice was open. Whether this is an adequate reply to Black depends on whether consistency conditions may rule out interventions, or only rule out histories given the interventions. The essay takes the first reading, because that is the reading the formalism is built for. A reader who wants the second reading will regard the exclusion as a restatement of Novikov's principle in other words. The difference, if it is one, is that the excluded object is a map, specified in advance, rather than a solution discarded after the fact.

## 9. A last distinction

It is tempting to hear the result as a licence for time travel. It is not. The wormhole construction still needs exotic matter. The process matrix that violates a causal inequality still has no agreed laboratory implementation. The computational tameness result of Baumeler and Wolf, that classical deterministic closed curves of this kind are no stronger than UP intersect coUP, still stands, and it is a reason for caution in the other direction: these loops do not solve NP-complete problems efficiently. The sketch says something smaller, and more useful for the question Faye's entry leaves open. A logic that allows the effect to precede the cause can block bootstrap loops and inconsistency loops by a unique-completion clause, and that clause has a unitary dilation. The dilation is not a second consistency axiom. It is the same clause, read on rows of a unitary instead of on fixed points of a Boolean function.
