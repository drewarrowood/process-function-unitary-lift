Every process function lifts to a unitary process

A note on logical consistency of causal loops, and on what that consistency already buys once a source and a sink are allowed.

Abstract. A process function is a classical map from the outputs of several parties to their inputs such that every local deterministic intervention has exactly one fixed point. Baumeler, Costa, Ralph, Wolf and Zych showed that every such map extends to a bijection by adding a source and a sink, and left open whether the bijection quantizes to a valid unitary process. Daher Ahmed and Kunjwal conjectured that it does. This essay argues that it does. The dilation is the permutation unitary of that extension. If two output strings differ, some party disagrees in the output bit and agrees in the function value. In the source-to-sink contraction that party contributes the inner product of two distinct rows of a local unitary, which vanishes. The induced map is unitary. Instruments follow by Stinespring. The novel step is not a new dilation. It is the identification: the process-function clause is the cancellation. Consistency, in the fixed-point sense, is already a quantization condition. Embeddability in a spacetime is a separate question, and the cyclic case stays open.

1. Four demands

Backward causation is asked to do four jobs at once. A loop should not be a contradiction. It should not exclude contradictions by a rider that cannot be failed from inside the theory. It should be reversible if the laws are. And it should survive the passage from Boolean interventions to quantum instruments. The literature has answers to each, and they are usually presented as one answer. They are not.

2. Bilking

The linguistic objection is that a cause is, by definition, earlier than its effect. Dummett argued in 1954, and again in 1964, that this is a stipulation about the word, not a discovery about the world. Black's bilking argument is the better objection. If an earlier event is supposed to be the effect of a later one, an agent who sees the earlier event can intervene so as to prevent the later one. Either the agent cannot intervene, which wants an explanation that is not itself the paradox, or the later event was not necessary for the earlier one. Lewis's reply splits the modal. One can, relative to the local facts and the laws. One cannot, relative to the whole past. That dissolves a verbal paradox. It does not say which histories the laws admit. Faye's Stanford entry, substantively revised on 28 October 2025, still treats the consistency paradoxes as unresolved in this sense.

3. Two paradoxes

The grandfather paradox is the empty case. An intervention is representable, and the composed map has no fixed point. The bootstrap is the overfull case. A trajectory, or a piece of information, is its own source, and the equations admit more than one continuation. Novikov's principle blocks the empty case and leaves the overfull case as a multiplicity. A uniqueness clause blocks both.

4. The billiard

Gödel's 1949 solution has closed timelike curves. Morris, Thorne and Yurtsever showed that a traversable wormhole, if held open, can be converted into a time machine. Friedman and collaborators proposed in 1990 that only self-consistent solutions occur. Echeverria, Klinkhammer and Thorne integrated the hard-sphere billiard. Some data have one consistent continuation, some have two, some may have none. Earman's warning stands. A consistency condition imposed after the dynamics is not a dynamics.

5. Three quantum replies

Deutsch's model asks for a fixed point of the partial trace. A fixed point exists in finite dimension, at the cost of nonlinearity and cloning. Aaronson and Watrous put the power at PSPACE. Bennett, Leung, Smith and Smolin called that an artefact. Tolksdorf and Verch showed the Deutsch condition can be met with no closed timelike curve. Lloyd and collaborators replace the fixed point by post-selection. That is a simulation. The process-matrix formalism of Oreshkov, Costa and Brukner is the setting used here. The quantum switch is causally nonseparable without violating a causal inequality. Lugano violates one. The two phenomena are not the same.

6. Process functions

A map from outputs to inputs is a process function when every local intervention has exactly one fixed point. Of 256 binary two-party maps, twelve are process functions, and none is a cycle. Lugano is the three-party witness. Each input depends on the other two outputs. The image has four elements out of eight. Dourdent and collaborators restate the clause as a complete pairwise-exclusive event list. It does not say which lists embed in a spacetime.

7. The boundary

Theorem 2 of the 2019 paper extends every process function to an invertible one by a source and a sink. The form used here is the permutation unitary U of ket o, e to ket w(o)+e, o. A bijection is a unitary. Validity is the extra claim. Daher Ahmed and Kunjwal conjectured it on 30 September 2026 and proved it under two sufficient conditions this argument does not use.

8. Time symmetry, adjacent

Price argued that a time-symmetric ontology should be retrocausal. Leifer and Pusey replaced reality of the state with lambda-mediation and obtained a timelike Bell factorization. Maudlin replied that the operational time-reverse is not always an experiment of the same kind. This dilation is adjacent, not a solution. The objection that bare Lugano is not an involution is an objection to refusing the source and the sink.

9. The proof

Lemma. If w is a process function and s differs from s', some party disagrees in the output and agrees in the function value. Otherwise one local function fixes both strings.

Theorem, written. For any local unitaries, possibly on an ancilla, the source-to-sink operator is unitary. Instruments follow by Stinespring. The gram matrix factors across parties. Off the diagonal the lemma supplies a party with shift zero and distinct rows, so that factor vanishes.

Kernel-checked, Lean 4.34.1, no sorry: the disagreement lemma for bits and for a general finite alphabet, and the source-sink map as a bijection on bits. Not kernel-checked: the contraction against a general complex unitary, and Stinespring.

10. Why this is novel

The Lugano case was done in 2017. The 2019 paper left the quantum step open. The 30 September 2026 preprint conjectured the general lift and proved it under two sufficient conditions this argument does not use. The new step is that the forbidden double fixed point is the off-diagonal the contraction kills.

11. Embeddability

The gap is physical, not algebraic. A process function is a list of events. A spacetime is a metric. Nothing in the cancellation turns one into the other.

Three things have been called an embedding. A post-selected circuit can make the Lugano event list appear in the runs that survive. That is a simulation. The source-and-sink bijection embeds the function in a larger function. That is reversibility. The 2019 paper realises it by interactions among bounded regions. It does not exhibit a metric. A Lorentzian embedding would be a local flow on a fixed metric whose solutions, for every local intervention, are exactly the fixed points of the process function. Only the third is the gap.

The standard wormhole does not close it. Echeverria, Klinkhammer and Thorne found data with one continuation, data with two, and data with none apparent. A process function demands exactly one fixed point for every intervention. The billiard has the wrong multiplicity. Deutsch keeps the multiplicity and averages it. Post-selection throws the extra solutions away. Neither is an embedding. Lugano is two-to-one on its original alphabet, so the relation is not the graph of a reversible Hamiltonian flow on those three bits. The source and the sink restore a bijection. The extra coordinates are operational boundaries, not a throat.

The ordered case does close. The twelve binary two-party process functions are channels. A channel embeds in Minkowski spacetime as free propagation from the earlier party to the later one. No wormhole is required. Baumeler and Wolf proved that a classical violation of a causal inequality needs three parties, so every two-party process function is of this kind.

The cyclic case does not close by the same route. Lugano violates a causal inequality, so no wiring in a definite order, and no mixture of orders, reproduces it if the settings are free. A metric with closed timelike curves can, in principle. What is missing is a local Hamiltonian on such a metric whose consistency equation is the process-function equation and whose multiplicity is one for every intervention. The billiard is a local Hamiltonian and has the wrong multiplicity. No other one is known. Adding a source and a sink does not supply the metric. It supplies two extra regions. Dourdent and collaborators leave the physical side open on purpose.

So the gap splits. Ordered process functions embed as channels. Cyclic ones have a reversible extension, and a written unitary purification, and no known local flow on a wormhole whose solutions are exactly their fixed points. The purification is a third object, beside the simulation and the embedding. The attempt does not close the cyclic case.

12. What else is not claimed

Freedom is not settled. The Leifer-Pusey no-go is not answered. Purifiability of every extensibly causal process remains open. The written theorem covers deterministic classical process functions only.
